## Why Note-Taking Is Critical

Three modules depend on this skill:
- **Retell Lecture** — 2–3 questions, ~10–12 marks speaking + listening
- **Summarize Group Discussion** — 2–3 questions, ~15–18 marks speaking + listening
- **Summarize Spoken Text** — **1 question** (occasionally 2), ~10–15 marks listening + writing

In all three, the audio plays once. You cannot replay it. If you don't capture the content in notes, you cannot deliver it.

---

## The Core Rule: Never Do Both at Once

The most common mistake: trying to listen and write simultaneously.

When you do both at once, you can do neither effectively.

The method is strict alternation:

1. **Listen** for 3–4 seconds
2. **Stop listening** (mentally) — start writing
3. **Write** for 3–4 seconds
4. Stop writing — **start listening** again
5. Repeat

When you write, ignore the audio even if it's still playing. When you listen, stop your pen.

---

## What to Write

- 4–5 sentences for Retell Lecture (1-minute audio)
- 10–15 sentences for Summarize Group Discussion (3-minute audio)
- 4–5 sentences for Summarize Spoken Text

Each sentence should be 7–10 words — short phrases, not full grammar.

Write legibly. If you can't read your own notes, they are worthless.

There is no "important" keyword — anything from the audio that you capture is valid. The AI rewards content proximity, not specific keywords.

---

## Retell Lecture — Note Format

Label your noteboard and write sentences as they come:

```
- idle thoughts turn negative
- looking for problems turns you into one
- picking up ball doing something creative
- open cookbook mind focused on solving
```

When speaking, use this template (optional):
> "The lecture discusses [topic]. In the beginning, the speaker explained [sentence 1]. Later, the speaker said [sentence 2]. Here, the speaker also mentioned [sentence 3]. In conclusion, [sentence 4]."

The template words are not in the audio and don't contribute marks — they're scaffolding.

> **Refinement (post–Aug 2025):** [[retell-lecture]] now recommends a **three-column** board (topic / supporting points / conclusion) instead of a flat list, and **38–40 seconds** of speaking rather than 25–30 — the longer you speak, the more content the scorer can credit. Connect ideas with linking words; a keyword list scores low.

## Summarize Group Discussion — Note Format

Label three columns: **S1 | S2 | S3**

As each speaker talks, write their points in their column:

```
S1: studying online harder than expected
    no structured schedule, hard to focus
    professors not tech-savvy

S2: biggest issue lack of interaction
    social media distraction
    hard to shift to study mindset

S3: managing time is biggest problem
    binge-watch lectures on weekends
    frustrating when tech fails
```

When speaking, attribute each speaker: "Speaker one said... Speaker two mentioned... Speaker three explained..."

## Summarize Spoken Text — Note Format

Same as Retell Lecture but output is written, not spoken. After taking notes:
- Transform into full grammatically correct sentences.
- No spelling or grammar errors allowed (unlike speaking modules).
- Target 58–65 words (50–70 required).
- Use template only if below 50 words.

**Note density target (APEUni):** Aim for 5–6 key points — 1 topic phrase or sentence plus 4–5 main points. Expect roughly **1 main point per every 2–3 sentences** of audio. This gives a natural pacing guide while listening.

**Fallback rule (APEUni):** If it is too difficult to identify key points, write down at least 5 sentences you can understand, regardless of whether they seem "important." Partial content is better than a near-empty response.

**Daily practice target (APEUni):** 2–3 SST tasks per day.
