# Answer Strategy — All 22 Question Types

**Summary**: A single answer-strategy cheat sheet for every PTE Academic question type across the three parts. Each block: what a top answer does, the exact move, the bottom-band trap, and a link to the full method.

**Sources**: `PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf` (Pearson, official) plus the task deep-dive pages and section guides linked throughout.

**Last updated**: 2026-06-04

---

## The one rule that governs the scored tasks

Seven task types are **human-reviewed for Content** (Describe Image, Retell Lecture, Respond to a Situation, Summarize Group Discussion, Summarize Written Text, Write Essay, Summarize Spoken Text). For all of them the same principle decides your score:

> **Reformulate real content into connected sentences. Never fill a fixed template with keywords.** The engine scores *meaning*, not keyword overlap — a slot-filled frame lands in the bottom Content band even when the keywords are right, and memorised material is classed as irrelevant → **Content 0 → fluency and pronunciation are then not scored at all.**

Everything below is an application of that rule, plus the mechanical traits (Form, Grammar, Spelling) that sit underneath it. Trait maxima are official; per-exam question counts and marks are from [[pte-score-guide]] and [[scoring-strategy]].

**Legend:** *traits* = official scored traits (max). "Human-reviewed" = Content double-checked by a person.

## Part 1 — Speaking & Writing

### 1. Read Aloud
*6–7 questions · Speaking · Content (per-prompt) + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** every word spoken, smooth rhythm, content words stressed, pauses at punctuation.
- **Move:** use the 30–40s prep to read silently and mark pause points. Speak at a steady natural pace — punctuation = pause cues.
- **Trap:** stopping to re-say a misread word (kills fluency). Keep going; silence >3s ends the recording.
- *Low weight in Academic (~5–8 marks) but it builds the fluency habit.* → [[fluency-training]]

### 2. Repeat Sentence
*10–12 questions · Listening + Speaking · Content 3 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** the sentence reproduced in correct sequence, fluently. Content is a 3-band scale (3 = all words in order, 2 = ≥50%, 1 = <50%).
- **Move:** short sentence (5–6 words) → repeat 100%. Long sentence (11–13) → capture the first 60–70% and deliver it fluently rather than fumbling the whole thing.
- **Trap:** straining to recall every word and stalling. **Two-thirds fluent beats full-but-broken** — fluency outweighs completeness here.
- → [[fluency-training]] · [[speaking-modules]]

### 3. Describe Image — human-reviewed
*5–6 questions · Speaking · Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** names the single most significant trend, cites real numbers, draws a relationship between features.
- **Move:** 25s prep → find the key trend. Use a loose frame filled with *this image's* data; 30–40s; no opinions.
- **Trap:** reciting a memorised template = **Content 0** = fluency/pronunciation not scored. A list of disconnected labels caps you at Content 1.
- → [[describe-image]]

### 4. Retell Lecture — human-reviewed
*2–3 questions · Listening + Speaking · Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** topic + 2–3 connected points + conclusion, paraphrased, with linking words.
- **Move:** three-column notes (topic / points / conclusion); read the on-screen image before the audio to pre-load vocabulary; speak **38–40s**.
- **Trap:** dumping isolated keywords → Content 1–3. Going silent mid-answer (use bridge phrases instead).
- → [[retell-lecture]]

### 5. Answer Short Question
*5–6 questions · **Listening** (not Speaking) · Vocabulary 1/0*
- **Top answer:** one correct word or short phrase.
- **Move:** listen for the single fact asked, answer in 1–3 words. It scores **Listening** — a correct one-word answer is a free Listening point.
- **Trap:** blind-skipping (you forfeit easy points) or overthinking a simple question.
- → [[speaking-modules]]

### 6. Summarize Group Discussion — human-reviewed
*2–3 questions · Listening + Speaking · Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** topic + each speaker's points + **synthesis** (who agrees/disagrees) + conclusion.
- **Move:** S1/S2/S3 notes; intro + 3 points per speaker + conclusion; speak 90–120s; never self-correct.
- **Trap:** listing speakers without relating them caps you at Content 4. Template use can trigger a human flag. Misattributing who-said-what is *not* penalised — only relevance is.
- → [[summarize-group-discussion]]

### 7. Respond to a Situation — human-reviewed (new, Aug 2025)
*2–3 questions · Speaking · Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5*
- **Top answer:** relevant **and** sufficient — accomplishes the goal in appropriate, reformulated language.
- **Move:** decode **Situation / Person / Goal**, then **SIMPLE** (Greet, Introduce topic, Mention problem, Politely ask, Lay option, End). First person; modal verbs (could/would/might).
- **Trap:** memorised response = Content 0; parroting the prompt's words; relevant-but-insufficient (no clear request stated).
- → [[respond-to-a-situation]]

### 8. Summarize Written Text — human-reviewed
*2 questions · Reading + Writing · Content 4 + Form 1 + Grammar 2 + Vocabulary 2*
- **Top answer:** ONE grammatical sentence, 5–75 words, capturing the main idea + 3–4 key points.
- **Move:** pick 3–4 key sentences (topic/conclusion, not examples) → join with comma + a **real conjunction** (and/but/while/because) → swap 2–3 words for synonyms → one full stop. Aim 60–65 words.
- **Trap:** multiple full stops or >75 words → **Form 0 → zeroes Grammar and Vocabulary too.** Comma-splicing with *however/therefore/moreover* (those are adverbs, not conjunctions).
- → [[swt-technique]]

### 9. Write Essay — human-reviewed
*1 question · Writing · Content 6 + Form 2 + Dev/Structure/Coherence 6 + Grammar 2 + General Linguistic Range 6 + Vocabulary Range 2 + Spelling 2*
- **Top answer:** 200–300 words, four paragraphs, one fully-developed idea per body, every part of the prompt answered.
- **Move:** identify the essay type → intro (paraphrase + position) → body 1 (main → support → example → mini-conclusion) → body 2 → conclusion. Simple correct words beat fancy wrong ones; no contractions.
- **Trap:** <120 or >380 words → Form 0; off-topic or memorised → Content 0; a multi-part prompt with one part missed.
- → [[essay-writing]] · [[essay-sample-questions]]

## Part 2 — Reading

### 10. Fill in the Blanks — Reading & Writing (Dropdown)
*5–6 questions · Reading (+Writing in Core) · partial credit per blank*
- **Top answer:** every dropdown set to the word that fits grammar **and** collocation.
- **Move:** skim meaning (10s) → grammar eliminates 2–3 options (part of speech: after *will* → base verb; after *the/a* → noun) → collocation picks the survivor.
- **Trap:** re-reading the whole passage per blank (too slow); ignoring collocation.
- → [[reading-fill-in-the-blanks]]

### 11. Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers (Reading)
*2–3 questions · Reading · +1 correct / **−1 wrong***
- **Top answer:** only the options you are confident are correct.
- **Move:** **negative marking** — a wrong tick cancels a right one. Tick only what you're sure of; when unsure, select fewer. Low weight, don't over-invest.
- **Trap:** ticking everything to "cover bases" → net zero or negative.
- → [[reading-modules]]

### 12. Reorder Paragraph
*2–3 questions · Reading · partial credit per correct adjacent pair*
- **Top answer:** the logical sequence; scored on adjacent pairs, so correct neighbours earn credit even if the whole order isn't perfect.
- **Move:** find the topic sentence (no *but/this/it* opener; introduces the subject) → trace pronoun/connector chains to link the rest.
- **Trap:** obsessing over a perfect full order instead of locking in correct adjacent pairs.
- → [[reading-modules]]

### 13. Fill in the Blanks — Reading (Drag and Drop)
*4–5 questions · Reading · partial credit per blank*
- **Top answer:** each gap filled correctly (more words are provided than gaps).
- **Move:** same grammar + collocation method; do the confident blanks first — it shrinks the option pool for the hard ones.
- **Trap:** starting with the hardest blank while the pool is still full.
- → [[reading-fill-in-the-blanks]]

### 14. Multiple Choice, Single Answer (Reading)
*2–3 questions · Reading · correct/incorrect*
- **Top answer:** the one correct option.
- **Move:** read the question first, then scan for the relevant line. No negative marking (single answer) → **always answer**. Low weight, don't burn time.
- **Trap:** deep-reading the whole passage for low marks.
- → [[reading-modules]]

## Part 3 — Listening

### 15. Summarize Spoken Text — human-reviewed
*1 question · Listening + Writing · Content 4 + Form 2 + Grammar 2 + Vocabulary 2 + Spelling 2*
- **Top answer:** 50–70 words of meaningful, connected sentences with clean grammar and spelling.
- **Move:** 5s-listen / 5s-write notes → 4–5 real phrases → drop into a light template → fix grammar freely (you may add/alter words). Aim 55–65 words.
- **Trap:** a keyword template = meaningless sentences = bottom Content band (**this was your exam feedback**). Grammar/spelling errors sink the mechanical traits, which are 6 of the 12 marks.
- → [[sst-technique]]

### 16. Multiple Choice, Multiple Answers (Listening)
*2–3 questions · Listening · +1 correct / **−1 wrong***
- **Top answer:** only confident selections.
- **Move:** **negative marking** (same as the reading version). Let the audio play, pick only what you're sure of.
- **Trap:** over-selecting → net negative.
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 17. Fill in the Blanks — Type In (Listening)
*2–3 questions · Listening · 1 per correct word spelled correctly*
- **Top answer:** every missing word typed and spelled correctly.
- **Move:** skim the transcript before the audio; follow along; jot blanks as you hear them on the noteboard; then type. Spelling **is** the mark.
- **Trap:** misspelling a word you heard correctly = lost mark.
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 18. Highlight Correct Summary
*2–3 questions · Listening + Reading · correct/incorrect*
- **Top answer:** the summary option that matches the audio's gist.
- **Move:** you can't read every option while listening — focus on the audio's overall meaning, then match afterward. Low weight.
- **Trap:** trying to read the summaries during the audio (you'll lose both).
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 19. Multiple Choice, Single Answer (Listening)
*2–3 questions · Listening · correct/incorrect*
- **Top answer:** the one correct option.
- **Move:** skim the question, listen for the relevant point, answer. No negative marking → **always answer**. Low weight.
- **Trap:** over-investing time for low marks.
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 20. Select Missing Word
*1–2 questions · Listening · correct/incorrect*
- **Top answer:** the word/phrase that replaces the final beep.
- **Move:** track the logical flow to the end; the missing word completes the meaning. Low weight.
- **Trap:** spending time here that belongs to Write from Dictation.
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 21. Highlight Incorrect Words
*2–3 questions · Listening + Reading · +1 correct / **−1 wrong** per word*
- **Top answer:** click exactly the transcript words that differ from the audio.
- **Move:** **negative marking** — follow the text with your cursor; click only words you're sure differ. **Missing** a word doesn't hurt; clicking a **correct** word does. ~12–15 marks — worth doing carefully.
- **Trap:** guessing clicks.
- → [[listening-modules]]

### 22. Write from Dictation — highest single-module score
*3–4 questions · Listening + Writing · 1 per correct word spelled correctly*
- **Top answer:** the sentence typed accurately — every correct word scores in **both** Listening and Writing.
- **Move:** shorthand notes while listening → expand immediately → add safe extra words (articles, prepositions). Extra words don't deduct; only missing/misspelled words lose marks.
- **Trap:** misspelling; relying on memory without notes for 12–15 word sentences.
- → [[write-from-dictation]]

## Effort priority (where strategy pays back most)

From [[scoring-strategy]], concentrate practice on the high-yield tasks:
1. **Write from Dictation** (#22) — biggest single contributor
2. **Repeat Sentence** (#2) and **Reading FITB** (#10, #13)
3. **Write Essay** (#9), **Summarize Group Discussion** (#6)
4. **SST** (#15), **SWT** (#8), **Retell Lecture** (#4), **Describe Image** (#3)

The skip-tier multiple-choice items (#11, #16, #18, #19, #20) are low weight — answer them quickly and respect the negative marking, then return your time to the list above. **Answer Short Question (#5) is not a true skip** — it's a free Listening point when you know the answer.
