# Write from Dictation

**Summary**: The highest-scoring single module in PTE Academic — 35–40+ marks writing, 17–20 marks listening. Three-step technique to maximize accuracy.

**Sources**: `Crack PTE Exam in 1 Hour _ Full PTE Course 2026 _ 22 Modules Live Demo.md`, `3–15 Days PTE Study Plan.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-19

---

## Why This Module Matters

Write from Dictation contributes approximately:
- **35–40+ marks to Writing** (the single largest writing contributor)
- **17–20 marks to Listening**

3–4 questions per exam. Every word in every sentence is worth one mark each to writing and one to listening. Getting 8/10 words correct per sentence across 4 questions has massive score impact.

This module alone can make or break your Writing section score.

---

## The Task

A short sentence (3–5 seconds) plays once. You type it exactly into the response box. No replay. No hints.

---

## Three-Step Method

**Step 1 — Short notes while listening.**
As the audio plays, write in abbreviated form — 2–3 letters per word, not just first letters. Write fast. Example: for "education and professional training play a vital role" → write "educ profess train play vital role".

**Step 2 — Expand immediately.**
The moment the audio ends, expand your short notes into full words while your audio memory is still fresh. Do this before typing into the box.

**Step 3 — Add extra words.**
Add articles (a, the), prepositions (in, of, at), plural forms, past tense variants. Extra words do not deduct marks. Any word from the audio that appears in your response earns a mark — order matters too, but extra words are safe.

Example from source:
- Audio: "Guests who book a stay at our beach resort will receive a sunset sailing excursion."
- Notes: "Gst bk stay bch rsrt rcv suns sail excrsn"
- Expanded: "Guest who stay at our beach resort will receive a sunset sailing excursion"
- With extras: "Guests who stays at our beach resorts will received a sunset sailing excursions"

Result: 14/15 marks (missed "book").

---

## Key Rules

- **Word order matters** — write words in sequence where possible.
- **Extra words are safe** — adding "the", "a", "an", "is" cannot hurt.
- **Spelling must be exact** — "recieve" instead of "receive" = 0 for that word.
- **No negative marking** — extra wrong words don't deduct; only absent correct words are lost.

---

## Common Sentence Lengths

- 8–12 words: most common
- Up to 15 words: possible but rare

For 8-word sentences, most candidates can retain the full sentence. For 12–15 word sentences, the three-step method is essential.

---

## What to Practice

- 20–25 questions per day (source recommendation)
- Use erasable noteboard in exam to write step 1 notes
- Work on spelling simultaneously — see [[scoring-strategy]] for why spelling is the #1 cross-module lever
