# Retell Lecture — Technique

**Summary**: Strategy for the Retell Lecture speaking task — the three-column note method, the pre-audio image trick, official scoring, and why keyword dumping fails after the August 2025 update.

**Sources**: `PTE 2026 Update! Retell Lecture Strategy That Actually Works!.md` (A One Australia PTE, YouTube transcript), `PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf` (Pearson, official)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-04

---

## Task at a glance

- Hear an academic lecture for up to **90 seconds**
- **10 seconds** to prepare, then speak for up to **40 seconds**
- **2–3 questions** per exam — PTE Academic
- Skills assessed: **Listening AND Speaking** — it pulls both scores up or down together, which is why it is high-value despite the low question count

## Official scoring (Pearson Score Guide)

| Trait | Max |
|-------|-----|
| Content | 6 |
| Pronunciation | 5 |
| Oral Fluency | 5 |

(source: PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf, pp. 21–24)

**Content is human-reviewed** before the final score is set. If Content scores 0, Pronunciation and Oral Fluency are **not scored at all** — the question contributes nothing. See [[pte-score-guide]].

## The August 2025 update: keyword dumping now fails

Pearson's official guidance is explicit: don't just list keywords. The scorer needs to hear **characters, actions, and the relationships between ideas** — not a word salad. After the update, content relevance is checked more strictly with human oversight (source: A One Australia PTE).

The official Content rubric confirms this:
- Score **6**: main ideas paraphrased seamlessly in own words, expanded with specificity, well connected with connective devices.
- Score **3**: "repetition of language from lecture without reformulation or context… mainly unconnected ideas."
- Score **1**: "repeats isolated words and phrases… does not communicate in any meaningful way."

A response that dumps remembered keywords lands at 1–3. A response that connects ideas with linking language lands at 5–6.

## The three-column note method

As the lecture begins, split your noteboard into three columns (source: A One Australia PTE):

| Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Topic / main idea | 2–3 supporting points or examples | Conclusion / implication / outcome |

Worked example. Lecture: *urban green spaces and mental health.*

```
Col 1: urban green spaces → mental health
Col 2: parks → less stress; 20 min nature → cortisol −15%; urban planning → commercial first
Col 3: govs need to legislate min green space
```

Rules:
- Use arrows, symbols, abbreviations. **Never write full sentences** — you are building anchors, not a transcript.
- **If a word is repeated or stressed, write it immediately.** Repeated/emphasised words are almost always the keywords the scorer rewards.

This three-column layout is a refinement of the linear note method in [[note-taking]] — it pre-sorts ideas into topic / detail / conclusion so your spoken response already has a shape.

## Three rules that hold under pressure

**Rule 1 — Read the image before the audio.** Most Retell Lecture questions show an image (diagram, photo, chart) before the audio starts. Use the ~3 seconds to read it and activate vocabulary about the subject, so your brain is warmed up when the lecture begins. (source: A One Australia PTE)

**Rule 2 — Never stop speaking, even if you lose your notes.** The recording cuts off after 3 seconds of silence. If you blank, use bridge phrases to buy a second without breaking fluency:
- "The speaker also mentioned several important factors relating to…"
- "It was further noted that…"
- "The overall message suggested that…"

These are fluency bridges, not content templates.

**Rule 3 — Aim for 38–40 seconds, always.** Pearson's guidance: the longer you speak, the more chances to cover the content requirements. A 22-second response, however accurate, gives the scorer too little material. Fill the time with connected, relevant ideas — even paraphrasing something you already said. (source: A One Australia PTE)

> Note: the older note in [[speaking-modules]] said "25–30s is enough." For Retell Lecture specifically, the post-update guidance is **38–40s**. Prefer the longer target here.

## High-scoring response shape

For the green-spaces lecture, a 6-Content response sounds like:

> "The lecture discusses the relationship between urban green spaces and mental health. Research from Australian universities found that people living near parks experience lower stress and anxiety. Notably, just 20 minutes in nature can reduce cortisol, the stress hormone, by about 15%. However, most cities still prioritise commercial development over green space. The speaker concludes that governments should legislate minimum green space in new housing developments."

It has a topic, two data points, a conclusion, linking language ("notably," "however"), runs ~38 seconds, and every idea ties to the actual lecture — not to a memorised frame.
