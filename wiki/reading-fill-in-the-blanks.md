# Reading Fill in the Blanks

**Summary**: The two FITB question types that together account for 50+ reading marks — grammar and collocation approach explained.

**Sources**: `Crack PTE Exam in 1 Hour _ Full PTE Course 2026 _ 22 Modules Live Demo.md`, `3–15 Days PTE Study Plan.md`

**Last updated**: 2026-05-19

---

## Two Variants

**Reading/Writing FITB (Dropdown)** — text up to 300 words, dropdown menus
- 5–6 questions
- ~25–30 marks reading (Academic)
- ~25–30 marks reading + ~25–30 marks writing (Core)

**Reading FITB (Drag and Drop)** — text up to 80 words, drag words from a box
- 4–5 questions
- ~25+ marks reading

Both use the same solving approach.

---

## Why Old Approaches Fail

The Reading section timer was reduced to 20–25 minutes. Reading the full passage top-to-bottom for every blank is too slow. You need to solve each blank in 30–60 seconds using grammar and collocation, not full comprehension.

---

## The Solving Method

### Step 1 — Skim overall meaning (10 seconds)
Get a general sense of what the passage is about. This prevents completely wrong word choices.

### Step 2 — Grammar approach per blank
Identify the grammatical role of the missing word:
- What part of speech fits? (noun, verb, adjective, adverb)
- After "will" → base verb
- After "the" or "an" → noun or adjective
- After a noun → adjective or verb
- Modify a verb → adverb (often ends in -ly)

Eliminate options that don't fit grammatically. This alone usually eliminates 2–3 of 4 options.

### Step 3 — Collocation approach
Of the remaining options, choose the one that forms a natural word pair with the surrounding words.

Examples from source:
- "generating ___ amounts" → "massive amounts" (collocation: massive amounts, not enormous or large amounts in this context)
- "strike a ___" → "strike a balance" (fixed collocation)
- "robust ___" → "robust safeguarding" (not exploitation — context of data breach protection)

### Step 4 — Easy first, hard last
In the drag-and-drop variant, solve the blanks you're confident about first. Return to difficult ones after placing easy words (fewer remaining options makes the hard ones easier).

---

## Grammar Reference (Common Patterns)

| Context | What to look for |
|---|---|
| After "will" | Base verb (hunt, pick, take) |
| After article (a/the) | Noun or noun phrase |
| Modifies verb | Adverb (-ly) |
| Modifies noun | Adjective |
| Collocation verb + noun | "strike a balance", "take notes", "pay attention" |

---

## Collocation Practice

Collocations are fixed word pairs that native speakers use together. They cannot be derived from grammar alone — they must be learned.

Examples:
- "blow one's top" = become angry
- "massive amounts" (not "big amounts")
- "strike a balance" (not "reach a balance" in formal text)

Source recommends: 10–15 min of collocation exercises daily in a practice app (vocabulary + collocations sections).

---

## Reading Skill Underlies It All

Grammar and collocation approach gets you partial marks. But the real ceiling is reading skill — how fast you read and understand context.

One hour of daily reading practice is the source's non-negotiable recommendation. Speed reading (using a rapid reader tool at 290–300 wpm) trains both speed and comprehension.

Without underlying reading improvement, FITB scores plateau.
