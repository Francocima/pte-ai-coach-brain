# Speaking Modules

**Summary**: All 9 question types in Part 1 (Speaking & Writing), with timings, scoring weights, and strategies.

## 1. Read Aloud

- **Prompt**: Text up to 60 words
- **Prep time**: 30–40 seconds
- **Response time**: Varies by text length
- **Skills**: Speaking
- **Marks (Academic)**: ~5–8 marks speaking only — near-dummy module
- **Marks (Core)**: ~30+ marks speaking + reading — very important

**Strategy**: Speak at a natural rhythm. No rushing. Use punctuation as pause cues. Stress content words. Pitch must stay consistent (no large variations). For Academic, don't over-invest time here.

---

## 2. Repeat Sentence

- **Prompt**: 3–9 second audio sentence
- **Response time**: 15 seconds
- **Skills**: Listening and Speaking
- **Questions**: 10–12 per exam
- **Marks (Academic)**: ~14–15 marks speaking, ~30 marks listening
- **Marks (Core)**: ~30 marks speaking, ~30 marks listening

**Strategy**: For short sentences (5–6 words) — repeat 100% exactly. For long sentences (11–13 words) — listen to only the first 60–70%, then speak those words fluently. Do NOT try to remember everything and fumble. Two out of three content is enough. Fluency matters more than content here.

See [[fluency-training]] for the foundational fluency method.

---

## 3. Describe Image

- **Prep time**: 25 seconds
- **Response time**: 40 seconds (25–30s is enough)
- **Skills**: Speaking
- **Questions**: 5–6 per exam
- **Marks (Academic)**: ~25–30 marks speaking

**Official traits**: Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5. Content is human-reviewed.

**Strategy**:
1. In 25 seconds, identify the key trends and the single most significant one.
2. Use the frame below as a loose scaffold — **fill it with the actual data of the image**, do not recite it verbatim:
   - "The image provided is a [bar graph / map / etc.] that gives information about [topic]."
   - "The X-axis talks about [X]. The Y-axis talks about [Y]."
   - Main details — 3 sentences citing real numbers and trends: "It is evident that... / Conversely... / Another interesting point is..."
   - Optional closing: "Overall, this image illustrates..."
3. Speak 30–40 seconds without stopping; use numbers; describe trends not labels; no opinions.

> ⚠️ **Memorised/templated responses score 0 for Content** (official), which also zeroes fluency and pronunciation. The frame is safe only when filled with this image's real content. Full method: [[describe-image]].

## 4. Retell Lecture

- **Audio**: Up to 90 seconds
- **Prep time**: 10 seconds
- **Response time**: 40 seconds (25–30s is enough)
- **Skills**: Listening and Speaking
- **Questions**: 2 (sometimes 3) — Academic only
- **Marks**: ~10–12 marks speaking, ~10–12 marks listening

**Official traits**: Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5. Content is human-reviewed (stricter since August 2025).

**Strategy**: Note-taking is the core skill. Use a **three-column** board (topic / supporting points / conclusion). Listen 3–4 seconds, write 3–4 seconds, alternate — never both at once. Read the on-screen image before the audio to pre-activate vocabulary. **Aim to speak 38–40 seconds** (longer gives the scorer more to credit). Connect ideas with linking words — keyword lists score 1–3 on Content.

See [[retell-lecture]] for the full method and [[note-taking]] for the note rhythm.

## 5. Answer Short Question

- **Prompt**: 3–9 second audio question
- **Response time**: 10 seconds
- **Skills**: **Listening only** (official — not Speaking)
- **Questions**: **5–6 per exam** (official; an earlier note said 3–4)
- **Scoring**: correct/incorrect — Vocabulary 1 (appropriate word) or 0
- **Marks**: low individual weight, but it scores Listening, not Speaking

**Strategy**: If you know the answer, say it in one or two words. If not, guess and move on. Low priority, but note it feeds **Listening**, so a correct one-word answer is a free Listening point — answer when you can rather than skipping blindly. (source: PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf, p. 24)

## 6. Summarize Group Discussion

- **Audio**: Up to 3 minutes (3 speakers)
- **Prep time**: 10 seconds
- **Response time**: 2 minutes
- **Skills**: Listening and Speaking
- **Questions**: 2 (sometimes 3)
- **Marks**: ~15–18 marks speaking, ~15–18 marks listening

**Official traits**: Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5. Content is human-reviewed.

**Strategy**: Label your noteboard S1, S2, S3. Take notes for each speaker the same way as [[note-taking]]. Intro + 3 points per speaker + conclusion. Speak 90–120 seconds; never self-correct. Top-band Content needs you to **synthesise the speakers' perspectives** (agreement/disagreement), not just list them. Misattributing who said what is not penalised — only relevance is.

See [[summarize-group-discussion]] for the full method.

## 7. Respond to a Situation

- **Prompt**: Text description of a situation, also read aloud as audio (same words)
- **Prep time**: 10 seconds (after the audio)
- **Response time**: 40 seconds
- **Skills**: Speaking (Academic)
- **Questions**: 2–3 per exam
- **Official traits**: Content 6 + Pronunciation 5 + Oral Fluency 5. Content human-reviewed; **memorised responses score 0**.
- **New task** — added to PTE Academic in **August 2025**.

**Strategy**: Decode three things from the description — **Situation, Person, Goal**. Then follow the **SIMPLE** order: Greet, Introduce topic, Mention problem, Politely ask, Lay an option (optional), End. Speak in the first person, use modal verbs (could/would/might), keep it relevant **and** sufficient. Reformulate rather than parroting the prompt's words.

> The older "use keywords directly from the question — no need to paraphrase" advice conflicts with the official anti-memorisation rule. Reformulate. Full method: [[respond-to-a-situation]].

## 8. Summarize Written Text

- **Prompt**: Text up to 300 words
- **Time**: 10 minutes
- **Response**: One sentence, 5–75 words (Academic) / multiple sentences, 25–50 words (Core)
- **Skills**: Reading and Writing
- **Questions**: 2 per exam

**Strategy**: Copy-paste method. Take sentence 1 from the beginning, one from the middle, one from the end of the source text. Join with commas and connectors (and, but, or). Put a full stop at the end. Target 60+ words for Academic. For Core, 40+ words. Do not paraphrase — exact text retained = higher content score.

---

## 9. Write Essay

- **Prompt**: 2–3 sentence topic
- **Time**: 20 minutes
- **Response**: 200–300 words (ideal: 260–270)
- **Skills**: Writing
- **Questions**: 1 per exam — Academic only
- **Marks**: ~26–30 marks writing

**Strategy**: See [[essay-writing]] for the full 10-hack breakdown.

Time allocation: Introduction 2 min → Body 1: 6 min → Body 2: 6 min → Conclusion 2 min → Proofread 4 min.
