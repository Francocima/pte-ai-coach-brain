# Summarize Written Text — Technique

**Summary**: Complete strategy for the SWT task — scoring breakdown, the copy-paste method, word count, vocabulary trick, and common failure modes.

**Sources**: `PTE Summarize Written Text Guide 2026.md` (OneAustraliaGroup), `PTE Writing Summarize Written Text Scoring Tips.md` (Edubenchmark), `PTE Writing_ Summarize Written Text Masterclass _ After Changes _ Tips, Strategies & Template.md` (Language Academy PTE), `Free PTE Exam Tutorials & Material.md` (APEUni), `PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf` (Pearson, official)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-04

---

## Task at a glance

- Read a passage (up to 300 words) → write **one sentence** summarising it
- 10 minutes per question; you get **2 questions**
- No rollover time — if you finish early, the spare minutes are lost
- Word limit: 5–75 words (PTE Academic); 25–50 words (PTE Core)
- Only one full stop allowed; no question marks, exclamation marks, or decimals

## Scoring (official — Pearson Score Guide)

| Component | Max | How it's graded |
|-----------|-----|-----------------|
| Content   | **4**   | Comprehensive summary, paraphrased, main ideas synthesised (4 down to 0) |
| Form      | 1   | Single complete sentence, 5–75 words |
| Grammar   | 2   | 2 = correct; 1 = errors but no hindrance; 0 = defective |
| Vocabulary | 2  | 2 = appropriate; 1 = lexical errors, no hindrance; 0 = defective |
| **Total** | **9** | |

(source: PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf, pp. 32–33; Language Academy PTE)

> **Correction (2026-06-04):** an earlier version of this table listed Content as max 2 (an out-of-date figure from the 2020 Edubenchmark source) and the components didn't sum to the stated 9. The official guide and Language Academy both confirm **Content is max 4**: 4 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 9. Content is the highest-weighted trait — cover 3–4 main ideas.

**Kill switch**: Zero on Form = zero on Grammar and Vocabulary too. Protect Form first.

**Human review:** SWT Content is one of seven task types double-checked by a human reviewer — irrelevant or memorised content is caught. See [[pte-score-guide]].

## Post-2025 changes

Two things changed after Pearson's update (source: Language Academy PTE):

1. **Human review added for content.** If your summary is irrelevant or incoherent, a human double-checks and can assign a low score. You can no longer write anything and get full content marks.
2. **Higher contribution.** SWT now contributes approximately 22 points to Writing and 18 points to Reading — roughly 40 points total. It is one of the most important task types in the exam.

## Sentence selection rules (APEUni)

Before connecting anything, filter which sentences are worth including:

- **Rule 1 — Topic relevance:** Identify the passage topic first. Any sentence not closely related to it is not a key sentence.
- **Rule 2 — No examples or elaborations:** Skip sentences that illustrate a point rather than make one.
- **Rule 3 — Prefer conclusions and topic sentences:** Choose sentences that are elaborated on elsewhere in the passage. Avoid sentences with repeated information.

(source: Free PTE Exam Tutorials & Material.md — APEUni)

## Conjunction warning

When connecting sentences, do **not** use transitional adverbs as if they were conjunctions. Words like *however*, *therefore*, and *moreover* are **not conjunctions** — they cannot link two sentences into one grammatically correct sentence on their own.

Incorrect:
> "Climate change is accelerating, however, governments are slow to respond."

The above has two independent clauses joined by a comma + adverb — this is a comma splice, which breaks Form.

Correct options:
- Use a coordinating conjunction: `, and`, `, but`, `, so`
- Use a subordinating conjunction: `although`, `because`, `while`
- Use a semicolon before the transitional adverb: `; however,`

(source: Free PTE Exam Tutorials & Material.md — APEUni)

## Recommended strategy: copy-paste + connector method

This is the Language Academy approach. It is faster, safer, and reliably covers content.

**Step 1 (1–2 min):** Read the passage. Identify 3–4 sentences that contain the most important keywords and ideas. Look for:
- The first sentence (almost always introduces the main topic)
- Sentences with proper nouns, technical terms, or repeated keywords
- A short sentence near the end if you are close to the word limit

**Step 2 (4–5 min):** Write the first selected sentence exactly. Do not put a full stop. Add a comma, then connect the next sentence:

```
[Sentence 1], and [sentence 2], furthermore, [sentence 3], moreover, [sentence 4].
```

Check your word count as you go. Stop adding sentences when you reach 60–65 words. Do not exceed 70.

**Step 3 (2–3 min):** Replace 2–3 words with synonyms to earn full vocabulary marks. Examples:
- *fight* → *combat*
- *preserved* → *stored*
- *humans* → *living beings*

**Step 4 (1 min):** Proofread. Check: one full stop at the end, no other periods, first word capitalised, no spelling errors. Do not capitalise a word just because it was the first word of a sentence in the original passage — you are writing one sentence, so internal capitalisation only applies to proper nouns.

(source: Language Academy PTE)

## Alternative strategy: bullet → compress

If the passage is complex and the sentences don't connect naturally, use this instead:

1. Write one bullet point per paragraph capturing its key idea
2. Compress all bullets into a single sentence using conjunctions
3. Aim for 35–50 words

(source: Edubenchmark)

## Word count target

| Source | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| Language Academy PTE | 60–65 words (more content, safer buffer before 75) |
| Edubenchmark | 35–50 words |
| OneAustraliaGroup | 35–50 words (ideal), up to 75 allowed |

**Recommended:** aim for 60–65. More content = higher content score. Staying 10 words below the limit also reduces the risk of accidentally exceeding 75.

## Templates (fallback when the copy-paste method doesn't fit)

Template 1:
> "The passage discusses [main idea] and highlights [supporting detail], emphasising [conclusion/impact]."

Template 2:
> "According to the passage, [main idea], while [supporting detail] illustrates [impact]."

(source: OneAustraliaGroup)

## Common mistakes

- Exceeding 75 words — zero for Form, zero for everything
- Using decimals (e.g. `3.5`) — AI reads the period as a full stop
- Writing multiple sentences — breaks Form score
- Capitalising mid-sentence words that were sentence-starters in the source
- Heavy verbatim lifting — risks low vocabulary score
- Choosing irrelevant or incoherent sentences — now reviewed by humans
- Using slang, abbreviations, or informal language
