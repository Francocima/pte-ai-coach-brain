# Summarize Group Discussion — Technique

**Summary**: Strategy for the SGD speaking task — the per-speaker note structure, the "structure not template" rule that avoids human flagging, official scoring, and what synthesis-level Content actually requires.

**Sources**: `PTE Summarize Group Discussion_ The ULTIMATE Blueprint for 90! (Masterclass).md` (Language Academy PTE, YouTube transcript), `Prepare for PTE Academic _ Summarize group discussion _ Everything you need to know 💡.md` (Pearson, official), `PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf` (Pearson, official)

**Last updated**: 2026-06-04

---

## Task at a glance

- Listen to a discussion between **three speakers**, 2.5–3 minutes, played **once**
- **10 seconds** to prepare, then speak for up to **2 minutes**
- **2–3 questions** per exam
- Skills assessed: **Listening AND Speaking**
- Discussions are academic: students debating a lecture, a professor and students on an assignment, friends on a topic. Speakers chop and change — they do not go neatly 1, 2, 3 (source: Pearson)

## Official scoring (Pearson Score Guide)

| Trait | Max |
|-------|-----|
| Content | 6 |
| Pronunciation | 5 |
| Oral Fluency | 5 |

(source: PTE-Academic-Test-Taker-Score-Guide.pdf, pp. 25–28)

Content is **human-reviewed**. Content feeds Listening; Fluency and Pronunciation feed Speaking. See [[pte-score-guide]].

## What full-mark Content actually requires

The official rubric is stricter than "mention each speaker." For a **6**, you must (source: Score Guide, p. 25):
- Paraphrase the main ideas in your own words and expand on each speaker's contribution;
- **Explore relationships between the points of view and synthesise the different perspectives** — note where speakers agree or disagree;
- Connect ideas logically with connective devices.

Score **4** is where most candidates land: "focuses more on individual perspectives than relationships between perspectives." Lifting to 5–6 is about **showing how the speakers relate to each other**, not just reporting them in turn (source: Pearson).

## The note structure: by speaker

Before the audio, label your board (source: Pearson):

```
Topic:
S1:
S2:
S3:
```

Leave space under each. As you listen, drop each point under the right speaker, plus their opinions and feelings. Track who's who as the conversation jumps around. Use keywords and shorthand — you can't write full sentences. This is the per-speaker variant of [[note-taking]].

## "Structure, not template" — avoiding the human reviewer

There is human intervention in SGD. If the system thinks you used a **template** or spoke off-topic, it routes your answer to a human examiner, who can mark harshly. To avoid being flagged: **maximise relevant content, minimise template** (source: Language Academy PTE).

Use a flexible **structure**, not a fixed template:
1. **Intro:** "The discussion was about [main topic]."
2. **Speaker 1:** one point, then "Furthermore… Moreover…" for 2–3 points.
3. **Speaker 2:** "After this, the second speaker mentioned… He also explained…"
4. **Speaker 3:** "However, the third speaker highlighted… He also provided…"
5. **Conclusion:** "Overall / To conclude, the discussion was about [topic]."

Vary your reporting verbs naturally (mentioned, explained, discussed, highlighted, elaborated) and link with connectors. Three to four points per speaker is the target (source: Language Academy PTE).

## Accuracy: relevance over precision

The scorer checks that you're **on topic**, not that every attribution is correct (source: Language Academy PTE):
- Mixing up which speaker said what is **fine** — no one verifies it.
- Saying "he" for a female speaker is **fine**.
- Minor factual inaccuracies are **fine** (if the audio says Earth is largest and you say Earth is largest but misattribute it, you're covered). What fails is reversing the substance — saying "Mercury is the largest" when the audio said it's the smallest.
- If you have **few notes**, don't panic — understand the discussion and rephrase the same ideas in different words to fill time.

## Delivery

- Speak **90–120 seconds** (at least 90). You don't need the full 2 minutes.
- Moderate, natural pace; natural pauses; clear over fast.
- **Never self-correct.** If you misspeak, move on — self-correction costs fluency marks.
- Don't worry about accent; focus on clarity (source: Language Academy PTE).
