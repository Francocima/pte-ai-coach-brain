## Scoring Criteria

| Criterion | Max | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Content | 6 | Fully addresses prompt, strong argument, specific examples, written in your own words |
| Development, Structure & Coherence | 6 | Clear intro/body/conclusion, logical paragraph order, wide range of connectives |
| General Linguistic Range | 6 | Wide vocabulary and sentence variety, very few errors |
| Spelling | 2 | Zero errors = 2; one error = 1; two+ errors = 0 |
| Grammar | 2 | Complex grammar used correctly, very few mistakes |
| Vocabulary Range | 2 | Varied vocabulary including idioms appropriate to the topic |
| Form (word count) | 2 | 200–300 words = 2; 120–199 or 301–380 = 1; outside that range = 0 |
| **Total** | **~26** | |

Essay contributes approximately 26–30 marks to Writing. Ideal word count: 260–270.

> **Form score also drops to 0** if you write in all capitals, omit punctuation, or use only bullet points / very short sentences. (source: pte-writing-essay-template-for-90-score.pdf)

## 4-Paragraph Structure

**Introduction** (2 sentences minimum):
1. Background/general statement — paraphrase the topic (not copy).
2. Paraphrase the actual question, changing key words.
3. State your opinion (if asked).
4. Optional outline sentence: "This essay will discuss..."

**Body Paragraph 1** — One central idea:
- Main idea (1 sentence)
- Supporting idea (1–2 sentences continuing the same idea)
- Example (1–2 sentences with "For example..." or "For instance...")
- Mini-conclusion (1 sentence tying back to main idea)

**Body Paragraph 2** — Second central idea (or counter-argument):
- Same structure: main → supporting → example → mini-conclusion.

**Conclusion** (2 sentences):
- Summarize body 1 and body 2 in a few words each.
- Restate your position.

## The 10 Hacks

**Hack 1 — Keep introduction short.**
Body paragraphs (2 and 3) drive your score. Don't spend more than 2 minutes on intro.

**Hack 2 — Use simple vocabulary.**
Do not use advanced or AI-sounding words (e.g., "ameiorated", "gregarious"). Use words you are comfortable with. Simple words used correctly outscore complex words used wrongly.

**Hack 3 — Spelling is binary.**
- 0 errors = 2/2
- 1 error = 1/2
- 2+ errors = 0/2
If unsure of a spelling, replace the word with one you know. Don't guess.

**Hack 4 — One idea per paragraph, fully developed.**
Do not list multiple ideas. Take one idea and develop it fully: main → supporting → example → conclusion. Think apple juice, not mixed fruit juice.

**Hack 5 — Cover every part of the question.**
Multi-part questions require each part addressed separately, even if in the same paragraph. Missing any part collapses content score.

**Hack 6 — No templates.**
Templates are detectable and will reduce your score. Use only linking words and skeletal structure (e.g., "On the one hand...", "For instance...", "In conclusion..."). The body content must be your own.

**Hack 7 — No contractions.**
"Don't" → "do not". "Won't" → "will not". Formal writing prohibits short forms.

**Hack 8 — Write formally.**
Do not write the way you speak. "They can rest also" → "This flexibility allows them to take breaks whenever they need, ensuring they can relax and recharge."

**Hack 9 — Structure before writing.**
After reading the question, immediately decide: what goes in body 1, what goes in body 2. This prevents mid-essay confusion and time loss.

**Hack 10 — Proofread the last 3–4 minutes.**
Reread word by word. Fix spelling and grammar. This alone can gain 3–4 marks.

## Essay Types

Five question types appear on the exam. Identify the type in the first 30 seconds — your structure depends on it.

### 1. Agree or Disagree
*"To what extent do you agree or disagree?"*
- Intro: Paraphrase topic, state your stance clearly, outline discussion.
- Body 1: First supporting argument + example.
- Body 2: Second supporting argument + example.
- Conclusion: Restate position, summarise reasons.
- Stick to one side throughout. Switching viewpoints hurts coherence score.

### 2. Discuss Both Views (+ your opinion)
*"Discuss both views and give your opinion."*
- Intro: Introduce both perspectives, state you will discuss both, hint at your view.
- Body 1: View A — reasons + example.
- Body 2: View B — reasons + example.
- Conclusion: State your opinion and justify it.
- Give equal weight to both views before declaring your stance.

### 3. Advantages and Disadvantages
*"Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of…"*
- Intro: Introduce topic, state you will cover both sides.
- Body 1: Advantages + examples.
- Body 2: Disadvantages + examples.
- Conclusion: Summarise both, optionally lean slightly toward one side.

### 4. Problem–Solution
*"What are the problems caused by X and what solutions can be suggested?"*
- Intro: Paraphrase the problem, preview discussion.
- Body 1: 2 main problems + examples.
- Body 2: 2 practical solutions + how they help.
- Conclusion: Summarise problems and solutions, end with a positive outlook.

### 5. Two-Part Question *(new type — not in older guides)*
*"Why is X happening? What can be done about it?" — two separate questions in one prompt.*
- Intro: Paraphrase topic, state you will answer both questions.
- Body 1: Full answer to question 1 + explanation + example.
- Body 2: Full answer to question 2 + explanation + example.
- Conclusion: Summarise both answers, strong closing line.
- Each body paragraph must answer its assigned question completely. Do not blend them.

(source: pte-writing-essay-template-for-90-score.pdf)

## Sentence Starter Templates

These are skeletal structures only — **not for copying word-for-word**. Pearson uses AI template detection; memorised sentences risk a low Content score or zero for the section. (source: PTE Essay Writing Guide _ PTE Writing Essay Template 2026.md)

Use the bracket placeholders as prompts, then write the actual content in your own words.

**Agree or Disagree**
- Intro: *"The question of whether [topic] has sparked considerable debate. I strongly believe that [position], and this essay will highlight the main reasons behind this view."*
- Body: *"To begin with, [argument 1]... For example, [specific example]... This clearly shows that [link back]."*
- Body 2: *"Moreover, [argument 2]... Studies have shown that [evidence]... This evidence underscores [connection to position]."*
- Conclusion: *"In conclusion, [restate position] because of [summary of reasons]."*

**Discuss Both Views**
- Intro: *"Some people believe that [view 1], while others argue that [view 2]. This essay will explore both perspectives before sharing my own opinion."*
- Conclusion: *"After weighing both sides, I believe that [your opinion] because [main reason]."*

**Advantages and Disadvantages**
- Intro: *"The topic of [subject] has both notable benefits and drawbacks. This essay will examine both to present a balanced view."*
- Conclusion: *"To summarise, while [topic] offers [key advantages], it also poses [key disadvantages]. Overall, [balanced statement]."*

**Problem–Solution**
- Intro: *"The issue of [problem area] has become increasingly concerning. This essay will explore the main challenges and suggest solutions."*
- Conclusion: *"In conclusion, while [problems] present major challenges, implementing [solutions] could greatly improve the situation."*

|---|---|

## Pre-Submit Checklist

1. Did you answer every part of the prompt?
2. Four paragraphs: intro, body 1, body 2, conclusion?
3. Ideas flow logically with linking words between paragraphs?
4. Examples, explanations, or facts support every main point?
5. Word count between 200–300?
6. No spelling errors? (replace any word you're unsure of)
7. No contractions (don't → do not)?
8. No all-caps, no bullet points, proper punctuation throughout?

(source: pte-writing-essay-template-for-90-score.pdf)

## Opinion vs. Both-Sides Questions

- "What is your opinion?" — Pick one side, defend it in both body paragraphs.
- "Discuss both views and give your opinion" — Body 1: View A. Body 2: View B + your position. Or Body 1: agree, Body 2: counter + refute.
- "Advantages outweigh disadvantages?" — Address both, then conclude which outweighs.

Always address both sides if the question raises two perspectives, even if you disagree with one.
