### What is the Summarize Spoken Text Task

The **Summarize Spoken Text (SST) task** kicks off the listening section of the [PTE Academic exam](https://www.pearsonpte.com/pte-academic). In this task, you’ll listen to a 60–90 second audio clip – often a lecture or interview – and write a concise summary of 50–70 words. The goal is to highlight the key ideas, demonstrating your ability to listen carefully and write clearly.

The purpose of SST is not to test creativity or advanced vocabulary. Instead, it evaluates your ability to identify key points, filter out unnecessary detail, and present information clearly and accurately under time pressure.

Let’s dive into the task format to better understand what’s required.

### Task Format and Requirements

Time is limited, and accuracy matters. You are expected to complete the task quickly while maintaining clarity and control.

- **Time:** Approximately 10 minutes
- **Word limit:** 50–70 words (strictly enforced)
- **Format:** One paragraph only
- **Input:** One audio recording (played once)
- **Skills assessed:** Listening comprehension, content selection, grammar, vocabulary, spelling

Exceeding or falling short of the word limit can directly reduce your score, even if your summary is otherwise strong.

### How This PTE Exam Task Gets Scored:

Your performance on the SST task is evaluated based on five key criteria:

- **Content:** Your summary needs to capture the main idea and key points from the audio. Avoid focusing on minor details or irrelevant information, as missing the main idea or going off-topic could mean no score.
- **Form:** The word count must be **50–70 words**. Falling outside this range could result in a lower score or even zero points.
- **Grammar:** Proper use of grammar, including verb tenses, punctuation, and sentence structure, is essential.
- **Vocabulary:** Appropriate vocabulary, along with effective paraphrasing, will enhance your score.
- **Spelling:** Accurate spelling is crucial, as errors can negatively impact your score.

The task uses a **partial credit scoring system**, so even if your response isn’t perfect, you can still earn points. However, no credit is awarded for missing the main idea or including irrelevant content.

Understanding these scoring criteria is key to excelling in this task.

### ❗ Important Warning About Templates (2026 Update)

Pearson has repeatedly warned candidates against using memorised templates. With increased use of AI-driven scoring and pattern detection, summaries that appear generic or mechanically structured can be strictly penalised.

Templates should never sound like they could apply to any lecture. If your summary feels vague, repetitive, or disconnected from the audio, it will not score well.

The safest approach is to use flexible structures, not memorised sentences. This will ensure that you will maximise your score, rather than risking everything.

### Summarize Spoken Text Template Structure:

The best templates follow a clear three-part structure:

**Introduction**, **Body**, and **Conclusion**. This format organises your ideas and ensures you cover all essential content.

- **Introduction Section**: Start with a brief statement that identifies the main topic or central idea. This should be concise – around 10–15 words – to set the context for the summary.
- **Body Section**: This is where you present the key points, arguments, and supporting details. Use about 30–45 words to focus on two or three main ideas that directly support the topic.
- **Conclusion Section**: Wrap up with a closing statement that highlights the overall message or final thoughts. Keep this section around 10–15 words to summarise the speaker’s main takeaway.

This structure ensures clarity and makes it easier to incorporate smooth transitions.

### Recommended Summarize Spoken Text Templates

Below are **three proven templates** you can use directly or adapt to your needs.

#### Template 1: Simple and Straightforward Summary

**Introduction:**  
The lecture revealed information about **\[Main Topic\]**. The speaker further elaborated about \_\_\_\_\_.

**Body:**  
Moreover, \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_ were also highlighted. Furthermore, \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ were also mentioned. In addition, \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_, and \_\_\_\_\_ were the main elements of the lecture.

**Conclusion:**  
Overall, it was an \_\_\_\_\_ lecture.

#### Template 2: Detailed and Analytical Summary

**Introduction:**  
The speaker provided the information regarding **\[Main Topic\]**. The speaker identifies \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_, also \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_ as the most important elements of the topic.

**Body:**  
Furthermore, the highlighted part from the lecture was \_\_\_\_\_. Other key ideas like \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_ were also explained in detail. Next, from the lecture, I could also hear the information about \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_.

**Conclusion:**  
Overall, the lecture suggested highlighting the facts about **\[Main Topic\]**.

#### Template 3: Emphasis on Key Points and Depth

**Introduction:**  
The speaker gave the information about **\[Main Topic\]**. The speaker draws attention towards \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_, also \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_ as the most crucial elements of the topic.

**Body:**  
Moreover, the speaker had also shed some light on \_\_\_\_\_. Furthermore, \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_, \_\_\_\_\_ and \_\_\_\_\_ were also described in detail. From the lecture, it was quite obvious that the speaker had emphasized \_\_\_\_\_, ***,*** and \_\_\_\_\_.

**Conclusion:**  
Overall, it was a very detailed/informative lecture based on **\[Main Topic\]**.

### Sample Sentence Starters and Connecting Words

Using effective sentence starters and transitions can improve the flow of your summary and demonstrate strong command of academic English. Here are some useful phrases for each section:

- **Introduction**:  
	• “The speaker discussed…”  
	• “The audio focuses on…”  
	• “The lecture introduced the topic of…”  
	• “The speaker compared… and…, exploring their differences and similarities.”  
	• “The audio addressed the issue of… and outlined possible solutions.”
- **Body**:  
	• “Additionally, he/she mentioned that…”  
	• “Furthermore, the speaker highlighted…”  
	• “Key points included… and…”  
	• “The speaker explained that… leads to…, providing examples like…”  
	• “Moreover, … and … were emphasised.”
- **Conclusion**:  
	• “Lastly, the speaker suggested…”  
	• “In conclusion, the audio underlined the importance of…”  
	• “Overall, the lecture summarised… and highlighted its significance.”

Including varied reporting verbs like “explained”, “highlighted”, “mentioned”, or “advocated” adds depth to your summary. Transitional words such as “however”, “therefore”, and “as a result” help maintain coherence.

### How to Modify the Template

While templates offer a reliable structure, they should be flexible enough to adapt to different types of audio content. Here’s how you can adjust the template for specific scenarios:

- **Cause-and-Effect Audio**: Clearly outline the causes and their effects. Use phrases like “The speaker explained that… leads to…” to connect ideas.
- **Comparison-and-Contrast Content**: Highlight both similarities and differences. Use connectors such as “They noted that… differs from… in terms of…, while both share similarities in…”
- **Problem-Solution Formats**: Present the issue and potential solutions. For example, “The speaker identified challenges like… and…, stressing their impact”, followed by “Possible solutions included…”
- **Informational Audio**: Stick to the basic template. Focus on identifying the main topic, two or three supporting points, and a concise conclusion.

Practising these adjustments will help you summarize effectively across a variety of PTE exam scenarios. By tailoring your approach to the content, you’ll ensure your summaries are both accurate and coherent.

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Avoiding common errors is just as important as including the right elements. Here’s what to watch out for:

- **Missing the main idea**: This is a critical mistake. Some test-takers get caught up in minor details or examples and lose sight of the overall message. Always ask yourself, “What is the speaker’s main argument?” If you can’t answer this, revisit the audio during practice sessions.
- **Violating the word count**: Writing fewer than 50 words or exceeding 70 can cost you points, no matter how well-written your summary is. Practise often to develop a sense of what the word limit feels like, and always double-check your count before submitting.
- **Including irrelevant details**: Resist the urge to include every example or background point from the audio. Your job is to summarize, not transcribe. Stick to the key message and supporting details that directly relate to it.
- **Grammar and spelling mistakes**: These errors can drag down your score. Develop a habit of active listening, taking concise notes, and proofreading your summary for errors before submission.
- **Poor time management**: Spending too much time listening and not enough time writing or reviewing can lead to rushed, error-filled responses. Allocate your time wisely during the task.
- **Over-reliance on exact wording**: Copying phrases directly from the audio shows limited vocabulary and and weak paraphrasing skills.

Having a dependable template can make summarising audio content much easier, especially when you’re working within the 50–70 word limit. This structured approach not only simplifies the process but also helps improve your PTE performance by ensuring you capture all the key points.

### FAQs – Summarize Spoken Text

#### What’s the best way to manage time during the Summarize Spoken Text task in the PTE exam?

To make the most of your 10 minutes in the Summarize Spoken Text task, it’s important to plan your time wisely. Start by spending about 2 minutes listening closely to the audio while jotting down brief, clear notes. Then, dedicate 3-4 minutes to organising those notes and outlining your summary. Finally, use the remaining 2-3 minutes to write your response and check for errors.

Practising regularly will sharpen your timing and build your confidence in handling the summarize spoken text task effectively.

#### How can I improve my listening and note-taking skills to summarize audio more effectively in the PTE exam?

To sharpen your listening and note-taking skills for the PTE exam, focus on active listening. Instead of trying to write down every single detail, pay attention to the main ideas and key points. Use abbreviations or shorthand to save time, and organise your notes with a clear system – think headings, subheadings, or symbols – to make them easy to follow later.

Make it a habit to practise with audio materials like podcasts or lectures. After listening, try paraphrasing what you heard in your own words. This approach not only strengthens your understanding but also helps you summarize information more effectively. With consistent practice, you’ll feel more confident tackling the Summarize Spoken Text task.
