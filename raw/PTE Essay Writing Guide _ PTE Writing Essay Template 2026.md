### What Is the PTE Academic Essay Task?

The PTE Essay task is part of the **Writing** section. You will be asked to write a 200–300 word argumentative or opinion-based essay.

#### Task Requirements

- **Time:** 20 minutes
- **Length:** 200–300 words
- **Type:** Argumentative, discussion, or problem-solution
- **Input:** A single prompt
- **Skills assessed:** Content, Development, Structure, Grammar, Vocabulary, Spelling

#### Common PTE Essay Question Types

1\. Agree or disagree  
2\. Advantages vs. disadvantages  
3\. Causes and solutions  
4\. Opinion with examples  
5\. Argument evaluation

## Main PTE Essay Types and How to Structure Them

Understanding the structure of each PTE essay type is essential for crafting clear, well-organised responses under exam conditions. Below are tailored structures for the most common PTE essay formats, designed to help you confidently approach each type and seamlessly integrate the templates discussed later.

### 1\. Agree or Disagree Essay

This essay type requires you to take a clear stance on a given statement. Examples of prompts include: *“Do you agree or disagree that social media has improved communication?”* or *“Technology has made life easier. To what extent do you agree?”*

**Basic Structure:**

- **Introduction:** Restate the topic and clearly state your position.
- **Body Paragraph 1:** Present your first supporting argument, backed by examples or evidence.
- **Body Paragraph 2:** Offer a second supporting argument, again with relevant evidence.
- **Conclusion:** Reaffirm your stance and summarise your main points.

Stick to a consistent position throughout your essay. Once you decide to agree or disagree, avoid switching viewpoints, as this can confuse the scoring system and hurt your coherence score.

### 2\. Discuss Both Views and Give Your Opinion Essay

For this type, you’ll explore two opposing perspectives and then share your personal opinion. A typical prompt might be: *“Some people believe online learning is superior to classroom education, while others disagree. Discuss both views and give your opinion.”*

**Basic Structure:**

- **Introduction:** Introduce the topic and explain that you’ll discuss both perspectives before sharing your view.
- **Body Paragraph 1:** Outline the first viewpoint, supported by reasons or examples.
- **Body Paragraph 2:** Present the opposing perspective, highlighting its strengths.
- **Conclusion:** Clearly state your opinion and explain why you lean towards this perspective.

Balance is key here. Treat both views with equal attention before revealing your stance. Ensure your opinion is well-supported and logically justified.

### 3\. Advantages and Disadvantages Essay

This essay type asks you to evaluate both the positive and negative aspects of a topic. Prompts may include: *“Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of working from home”* or *“What are the benefits and drawbacks of living in a large city?”*

**Basic Structure:**

- **Introduction:** Introduce the topic and mention that you’ll explore both advantages and disadvantages.
- **Body Paragraph 1:** Discuss the main advantages, using specific examples.
- **Body Paragraph 2:** Address the disadvantages, supported by clear details.
- **Conclusion:** Summarise both sides and, if appropriate, indicate a slight preference.

The goal is to remain objective while showcasing critical thinking. You can lean slightly towards one side in the conclusion, but avoid being overly biased.

### 4\. Problem-Solution Essay

In this format, you’ll identify key issues and propose actionable solutions. Prompts might include: *“What are the main environmental problems facing cities today, and how can they be addressed?”* or *“Discuss the challenges of an ageing population and suggest possible solutions.”*

**Basic Structure:**

- **Introduction:** Briefly outline the issue and preview the problems and solutions you’ll discuss.
- **Body Paragraph 1:** Describe the main problems, supported by evidence or examples.
- **Body Paragraph 2:** Propose practical solutions, explaining how they could be implemented.
- **Conclusion:** Highlight the importance of addressing these issues and reinforce your proposed approach.

Focus on realistic, actionable solutions that directly address the problems. Avoid overly idealistic ideas that lack practicality or feasibility.

Each essay type follows a logical flow, ensuring your argument is easy to follow. The PTE scoring system rewards clarity and strong organisation, so using the right structure for each essay type is critical to achieving a high score. These frameworks provide a solid foundation for adapting your responses during the exam.

## Ready-to-Use Essay Templates

These templates align with the exam structures discussed earlier, helping simplify your writing process. They offer a clear format that meets PTE scoring standards while maintaining flexibility for different essay prompts.

### How to Use and Adapt Templates

Once you’re familiar with the essay structures, the next step is learning how to customise these templates. Think of templates as blueprints you can tailor to suit any essay topic. The trick is to slot in your content while keeping the structure intact.

Begin by identifying the essay type based on the prompt. Look for keywords like “agree or disagree”, “discuss both views”, “advantages and disadvantages”, or “problems and solutions.” After identifying the type, pick the corresponding template and adjust it to fit your topic.

Start with the introduction by rephrasing the prompt and including the specific topic. Avoid copying the question directly; instead, paraphrase it to showcase your vocabulary skills. For instance, if the prompt is about “social media’s impact on communication”, you could write “the influence of online platforms on human interaction.”

For the body paragraphs, the templates guide you with connecting phrases and structure, but you’ll need to add your arguments, examples, and evidence. This approach not only saves time but also ensures your essay stays organised and coherent.

Use natural, conversational language. Essays that sound authentic score better than ones filled with mechanical or repetitive phrasing. Let the template structure guide you, but make sure your unique voice and ideas shine through in the examples and arguments you choose.

Practise using these templates under timed conditions before exam day. The more you practise, the more confident and efficient you’ll become.

### Templates for Each Essay Type:

Here are simplified templates for common essay types:

#### 1\. Agree or Disagree Template:

*Introduction:* “The question of whether \[restate topic\] has sparked considerable debate. I strongly believe that \[state your position\], and this essay will highlight the main reasons behind this view.”

*Body Paragraph 1:* “To begin with, \[main argument 1\] provides strong support for this perspective. For example, \[specific example or evidence\]. This clearly shows that \[explain how the example backs your argument\].”

*Body Paragraph 2:* “Moreover, \[main argument 2\] further strengthens this stance. Studies have shown that \[evidence or example\]. This evidence underscores \[connection to your main position\].”

*Conclusion:* “In conclusion, \[restate your position\] because of \[briefly summarise main arguments\]. The points discussed strongly reinforce the idea that \[final statement supporting your stance\].”

#### 2\. Discuss Both Views Template:

*Introduction:* “Some people believe that \[first viewpoint\], while others argue that \[opposing viewpoint\]. This essay will explore both perspectives before sharing my own opinion.”

*Body Paragraph 1:* “Supporters of \[first view\] argue that \[main supporting point\]. They often cite \[evidence or example\] to back their stance. This view implies that \[explain implications\].”

*Body Paragraph 2:* “On the other hand, advocates of \[second view\] claim that \[main supporting point\]. They point to examples like \[evidence or research\]. This perspective highlights \[explain significance\].”

*Conclusion:* “After weighing both sides, I believe that \[your opinion\] because \[main reason\]. Although both arguments have merit, \[final justification for your stance\].”

#### 3\. Advantages and Disadvantages Template:

*Introduction:* “The topic of \[subject\] has both notable benefits and drawbacks. This essay will examine the key advantages and disadvantages to present a balanced view.”

*Body Paragraph 1:* “The main advantages of \[topic\] include \[advantage 1\] and \[advantage 2\]. For instance, \[specific example illustrating benefits\]. These benefits highlight \[broader implications\].”

*Body Paragraph 2:* “However, there are also significant disadvantages, such as \[disadvantage 1\] and \[disadvantage 2\]. For example, \[specific example illustrating drawbacks\]. These challenges reveal \[broader implications\].”

*Conclusion:* “To summarise, while \[topic\] offers \[mention key advantages\], it also poses \[mention key disadvantages\]. Overall, \[slight preference or balanced statement\].”

#### 4\. Problem-Solution Template:

*Introduction:* “The issue of \[problem area\] has become increasingly concerning. This essay will explore the main challenges and suggest solutions to tackle them.”

*Body Paragraph 1:* “Key problems include \[problem 1\] and \[problem 2\]. For example, \[specific example of problems\]. These issues have led to \[consequences or effects\].”

*Body Paragraph 2:* “To address these issues, several solutions can be implemented. Firstly, \[solution 1\] could help by \[explanation\]. Additionally, \[solution 2\] might \[explanation of its impact\]. These measures could \[expected outcomes\].”

*Conclusion:* “In conclusion, while \[restate main problems\] present major challenges, implementing \[mention solutions\] could greatly improve the situation. Taking action is crucial to \[final call to action\].”

### FAQs – PTE Essay Template

#### How can I effectively practise essay writing under timed conditions using these templates?

To get better at writing essays under timed conditions, start by getting comfortable with a basic essay structure – *introduction, body, and conclusion*. Break it into smaller pieces and practise each section separately until you feel at ease with the flow.

When you’re ready, try simulating exam conditions. Set a timer during your practice sessions to make sure you can finish an essay within the time you’re given. It’s also a good idea to spend a few minutes planning your response before you start writing. This helps you organise your thoughts and stick to your main points. Practising regularly with different mock prompts will sharpen your speed, accuracy, and ability to adjust your structure to suit various essay topics.

#### How can PTE essay templates improve coherence and cohesion in your writing?

PTE essay templates serve as a practical guide to help you organise your thoughts and ideas in a way that feels natural and easy to follow. With a clear structure in place, your arguments can flow seamlessly from one point to the next, making your essay more cohesive and easier to read.

These templates often include linking words and transitional phrases, which act as bridges between your ideas. This not only improves the overall connection between points but also ensures your essay stays on track and well-structured. Using such a framework can make a big difference in achieving higher scores in the PTE Academic writing section.
